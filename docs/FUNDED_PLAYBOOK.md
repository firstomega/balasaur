# The funded playbook (hypothetical)

Written 2026-08-13 as a thought exercise: what the plan becomes with Series A
money and a team of 25. **None of this is the current plan.** Balasaur is a
one-person project and `/CLAUDE.md` governs real decisions. Keep this for
reflection, or for the day the premise stops being hypothetical.

## What funding changes

Every recommendation elsewhere in this repo is shaped by four constraints:
one person, part time, no marketing budget, no social posting. Funding
dissolves all four, and the plan inverts:

- Hand-curation becomes affordable, so "the gate is the curator" stops being
  the only option for quality.
- Social becomes a channel, because someone else is doing the posting.
- Programmatic SEO drops from _the_ strategy to _one_ channel of five.
- The AdSense endgame dies. 25 people is roughly $5M a year of burn, which
  demands subscription plus data licensing, not display revenue.

What does not change: you cannot out-JustWatch JustWatch on availability or
out-IMDb IMDb on data. The wedge stays the decision layer, "is this good and
should I watch it tonight," and the Balasaur Score stays the only defensible
asset. Everything below serves making the score an authority people seek by
name.

## Foundation, weeks 0 to 8, before any acquisition spend

1. **Fix the data license.** Commercial TMDB, then evaluate Gracenote,
   Watchmode, or a direct JustWatch deal. At $149 a month the old blocker is
   a rounding error, and every channel below is legally fragile without it.
2. **Instrument before spending.** Product analytics, attribution, cohort
   retention. Buying users without D1/D7/D30 and CAC by channel is how seed
   money disappears.
3. **Make the score citable, not just visible.** Publish the methodology as a
   real paper, `AggregateRating` schema everywhere, pursue Wikidata
   notability as a rating source. Converts a feature into a brand asset that
   AI assistants and journalists quote by name.

## Product changes that unlock acquisition, months 2 to 6

4. **Native iOS and Android apps.** The biggest structural add. App Store
   search is its own discovery channel, push is the only owned
   re-engagement that works, and "track what I watched" is a phone behavior
   a website loses.
5. **Availability alerts.** "The film you saved is on Netflix as of today."
   The killer utility in this category and the real reason to hold an
   account. Turns the nightly pipeline into a user-facing product.
6. **The social layer.** Public profiles, followable lists, friend activity.
   The only genuine viral loop the category has, and essentially all of
   Letterboxd's growth. Build it before the audience so the audience
   compounds.
7. **Personalization that beats "popular."** The taste graph is the retention
   engine. Recommendations good enough to surprise people are what make the
   product worth returning to.

## Content and SEO at team scale, months 2 to 9

8. **Two or three editorial writers on top of the programmatic layer.** The
   hybrid is what survives core updates: programmatic breadth for the long
   tail, human depth on head terms.
9. **Internationalize.** Geo-ranking already works and incumbents are weak in
   DE, FR, BR, JP. The cheapest large traffic pool available.
10. **Own AI citation.** Structured data, a real `llms.txt`, and being the
    numerically citable source for "is X good." Early enough that a small
    team can still win it.

## Distribution beyond search, months 3 to 12

11. **The embeddable score widget, resourced properly.** How Rotten Tomatoes
    and Metacritic built their link graphs. With a BD person driving
    placements it becomes a channel, not a side project.
12. **Integrations and partnerships.** Plex, Trakt, Roku, smart TV portals,
    ISP landing pages. One deal can exceed a year of SEO.
13. **Creator program.** Pay the YouTubers and TikTokers already making "what
    to watch" content to cite the score. You are not posting; they are. That
    was the actual constraint, and it is purchasable.
14. **An annual data report.** "The State of Streaming Taste," built from the
    catalog. Data journalism earns editorial backlinks nothing else does,
    and it repeats every year.
15. **Paid acquisition, last and gated.** App Store ads and high-intent
    search only, and only once retention proves out. Weak D30 turns paid
    spend into a bonfire. Deliberately last.

## The failure mode to watch

A funded team in this category builds a better IMDb nobody asked for. The
discipline that prevents it is already written down: every surface names the
rent it pays. Capacity is precisely what makes that principle hardest to
hold.

Items 4, 6, and 7 describe a different company than the one running today.
They are right with funding and wrong without it.
