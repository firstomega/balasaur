SUMMARY:
The SEO plumbing is better built than the site's zero traffic suggests, and the thing holding it back is not missing infrastructure but two sitemaps that submit the wrong pages. The title sitemap in src/lib/media.server.ts caps at 2,500 URLs (SITEMAP_TITLE_BUDGET, line 28) and sorts by TMDB popularity, which is a measure of what is trending on TMDB this week, not of what anyone searches for. I ran the exact query the code runs against production: of the 2,500 URLs submitted, 896 are titles with under 50 ratings, while 4,749 titles with 1,000 or more ratings are left out entirely. The top twenty omitted, by rating count, are Guardians of the Galaxy (30,087 ratings), The Hunger Games, Captain America: The First Avenger, Jurassic World, John Wick, Logan, Get Out, The Empire Strikes Back and The Silence of the Lambs. The comment block at lines 503 to 509 states in capital letters that the list is "Ordered by RATING COUNT, not TMDB popularity" and cites the measurement that justified it; the comment at lines 526 to 528, eighteen lines below, says the opposite and the code does what the second comment says. The people sitemap has the same shape of bug: its loop stops at offset 5,000 and its header comment says "About 1,860 people at the current gate", but 10,663 people actually clear the 8-credit gate, so roughly 5,600 qualifying people pages are never submitted.

STRENGTHS:
- Canonicals are on every route that needs one, and slug drift is handled properly: src/routes/movie.$id.tsx line 60 and tv.$id.tsx line 60 issue a 301 to the canonical slug, and the sitemap emits the slugged URL directly so crawlers never pay for the redirect hop.
- One definition of "indexable", three consumers. The indexable_media view backs the noindex meta tag, the sitemap, and IndexNow pings, so a URL that renders noindex can never be submitted. That class of bug is structurally impossible here.
- The structured data is honest about its own limits in the one place it usually is not. src/lib/jsonld.ts lines 26 to 36 refuse to emit aggregateRating when the vote count is missing, with a note that a count-less fallback had put 11,366 pages in Search Console's invalid pile. Declining to mark up 11,000 pages rather than mark them up wrong is the right call.
- Paginated browse pages self-canonicalise instead of pointing at "/" (src/routes/index.tsx lines 131 to 138), and the crawl trail links page 1, the neighbours and the last page, so a crawler reaches the end of the catalog without walking a 167-link chain.
- Retired collection slugs 301 rather than 404 (src/routes/best.$slug.tsx lines 30 to 36), so the ~200 slugs renamed in the v8 media-type split keep whatever indexing they had earned.
- The collection gate holds in production: all 678 collections carry at least 10 items, minimum 10, maximum 60. No thin ranked pages are being submitted.

GAPS:
- The title sitemap submits 2,500 of 61,673 indexable titles, sorted by the wrong signal. 896 submitted URLs have under 50 ratings; 4,749 titles with 1,000-plus ratings are absent. This is the single highest-leverage fix on the site and it is a one-line change to the .order() call at src/lib/media.server.ts line 530.
- Two comments in the same function contradict each other and the code follows the wrong one. Lines 503 to 509 describe rating-count ordering as a decision already made and measured on 2026-08-14; lines 526 to 528 describe popularity ordering as the decision. Whichever landed second silently reverted the other. Nothing in the pre-ship gate caught it because the sitemap still rendered valid XML.
- The people sitemap's loop bound (offset < 5000) drops 5,663 of the 10,663 people who clear the 8-credit gate, and its header comment understates the qualifying population by more than 5x.
- 87% of the catalog has no crawl path from the browse surface. The homepage catalog is capped at 10,000 rows (CATALOG_LIMIT, src/lib/media.server.ts line 38) at 60 per page, so 53,571 indexable titles are not reachable by paging the homepage. 3,494 titles with 1,000-plus ratings are in neither the sitemap nor the browsable 10,000, and depend entirely on a person page or a collection happening to link them.
- The aggregateRating pairs a ratingValue that is the Balasaur Score, a blend of four external sources, with a ratingCount that is TMDB's vote count alone. The number and its count describe different populations. Nobody can reconstruct the value from the count, which is the Principle 3 test, and Google's rich-results reviewers read the pair as one claim.
- Everything above is a submission problem, not a crawl problem: robots.txt and the sitemap index both serve correctly, so the crawler is being pointed accurately at the wrong 2,500 pages.

METHOD:
Read src/lib/media.server.ts (listSitemapEntries), all four sitemap routes, robots.txt, src/lib/seo.ts, src/lib/jsonld.ts, and the head() blocks of the movie, tv, person, best and index routes; reproduced the sitemap's exact ORDER BY against production Supabase to count what it submits and what it omits, and read the omitted high-rating titles by name. Ran the dev server and confirmed /robots.txt and /sitemap.xml serve correct output. I could not check live Search Console coverage, actual Googlebot crawl behaviour, or the rendered detail pages, since this container cannot reach Supabase or balasaur.com.
