The house voice is real and mechanically defended: `src/lib/copyLint.test.ts` fails the build on an em-dash or on "powered by / seamlessly / curated / leverage" in any user-visible string, and it passes clean at HEAD (121 tests, 0 fail). STRENGTHS:
- The copy rule is a test, not a convention. src/lib/copyLint.test.ts walks every .ts/.tsx file, strips comments, and fails on em-dashes and four banned filler words in string or JSX-text position. `bun test src` at HEAD: 121 pass, 0 fail. Most projects write this rule in a doc and then break it.
- The prose generators refuse to emit weak sentences instead of padding. collectionsProse.ts drops the score-spread line under 8 items, drops the decade line unless one decade holds 40 percent or more, drops the streaming line unless one service carries 30 percent. titleProse.ts caps the paragraph at 5 sentences and will not call an 8 point source gap a disagreement.
- The catalog-median sentence was already recognised as a bad sentence and removed from title pages. The comment block at the top of src/lib/titleProse.ts says so explicitly: nobody holds a 66,000-title median in their head, so it was replaced with genre-and-decade cohort and franchise comparisons. That is principle 3 applied correctly, by hand, on the surface that matters most.
- Meta description ordering is deliberate and correct. collectionsProse.ts leads with the three named leaders and their scores, because that is the only part that differs between 678 collection pages; the "blends IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic, and TMDB" sentence was moved to last precisely so it stops filling every snippet with identical text.

GAPS:
- The catalog median is wrong by 4 points and it is on 633 live pages. src/lib/collectionsProse.ts:27 says 62; the real median across 61,673 indexable titles is 66. For the 56 collections whose median is 67 to 69, the sentence claims "well above" a one to three point gap. Best example: /best/best-horror-movies-on-hulu, median 67. Same 62 is hardcoded as the Bayesian prior at src/lib/rank.ts:24, so fixing one without the other splits them.
- Catalog size has three answers. about.tsx:60 "More than 66,000 movies and TV shows", llms.txt "66,000 titles", collections.tsx:30 "Ranked lists drawn from 65,000 titles". The media table holds 77,672 rows and indexable_media holds 61,673. Every one of the three published numbers is above the number of pages that actually exist. A single source constant would fix this permanently.
- "Balasaur learns your taste. It's free." at best.$slug.tsx:184 is a promise with nothing behind it. Grepping liked/disliked across src/ returns only profile.functions.ts (public profile display), $handle.tsx (a tab), and lists.tsx (bucket assignment). No ranking, no recommendation, no filter reads a sentiment. There are 2 profiles and 109 rows in user_media_status total, so the claim also cannot be checked by a visitor.

- methodology.tsx:55 says membership "favors titles with many ratings, utilizing a Bayesian prior." "Utilizing" is exactly the machine-voice register the lint exists to catch, and it is not in BANNED_WORDS. The word is "using."
- The collection dek never says how many collections exist, but the source comments say 635 twice while the table holds 678. Comments are not user-visible so this is not a copy defect, but it is the same drift that produced the three catalog counts: numbers written down once and never rechecked against the query.
