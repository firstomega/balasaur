SCORE_AGREE: no, I would give 6.5
The phone case is genuinely solid and defended by a CI gate; the two real defects are a tablet-width headline collision and an iOS zoom-on-tap that affects every input, neither of which justifies a midpoint score.

SUMMARY:
Nothing on this site scrolls sideways. I measured document width against viewport width on 10 routes at 8 widths (320, 360, 375, 390, 412, 768, 1024, 1440) and every combination came back at or under the viewport, with the home page at 320px the only one within 1px of the edge. That is not luck: scripts/layout-check.ts runs the same measurement on every pull request via .github/workflows/layout.yml and uploads a screenshot of each route at each width. The phone experience holds up in the rendered page too. The nav drawer opens to 260px, locks body scroll, closes on Escape, and its rows are 259 by 40 pixels. Two things are broken. Between 768px and roughly 830px the home page headline spills out of its own box and paints across the blue sign-in button: at 768px the text runs 59px past the right edge of the h1, at 800px 27px, clean from 840px up. That band is iPad portrait, the single most common tablet width there is. Separately, every text input the app wrote itself is between 11.5px and 14px, and iOS Safari zooms the whole page whenever a focused input is under 16px, so tapping search, the sign-in email field, or the Movie Night name field on an iPhone zooms in and leaves the user pinching back out.

STRENGTHS:
- Zero horizontal overflow, verified not assumed. 10 routes times 8 widths, every one at or below viewport width. The narrowest phone I tried (320px, iPhone SE first generation) came in at 1px over on home, which is rounding, not a bug.
- The overflow check is automated and blocks merges. .github/workflows/layout.yml runs scripts/layout-check.ts on every pull request and push to main, names the offending element when it fails, and uploads full-page screenshots of every route at 390, 768 and 1440 pass or fail. Most one-person projects have nothing like this.
- The mobile nav drawer is correct. At 390px it opens to 260px wide, sets body overflow to hidden so the page behind does not scroll, traps focus on a visible close button, and closes on Escape. Rows are 40px tall, which is close enough to thumb-sized.
- Search gets its own full-width row below the top bar on phones (TopBar.tsx:229, `md:hidden`), which is why the bar fits a 390px screen with a menu button, logo, Rate and Sign in and still leaves the search field 358px wide.
- /night, the feature shipped yesterday, is the best mobile page on the site. Three full-width option cards, each roughly 80px tall, nothing cramped, no overflow at 320px.

GAPS:
- The home headline collides with the primary call to action at iPad portrait width. In LandingHero.tsx:28-44 the text column carries `min-w-0` and the button column carries `shrink-0`, so at 768px the text column is squeezed to 108px while the word "entertainment" needs about 200px. The h1 has no word-break, so the word paints over the button. Measured spill: 59px at 768, 27px at 800, 7px at 820, gone by 840. The fix is one class on the h1 or dropping `shrink-0`.
- Every input zooms the page on iPhone. TopBarSearch.tsx:161 is 12px, AuthDialog.tsx:128 and :138 are 12px, FilterRail.tsx:1286 is 11.5px, night.index.tsx:193 and night.$code.tsx:344 are 14px, account.tsx:158 and profile.tsx:43 are 12px, play.tsx:128 is 13px. iOS zooms below 16px. The shadcn base component at src/components/ui/input.tsx:11 already handles this correctly with `text-base md:text-sm`, and none of the app's own components use it.
- Tap targets are consistently in the low thirties rather than the mid forties. At 390px: menu button 32 by 32 (TopBar.tsx:37, `h-8 w-8` with no padded hit area), Rate 72 by 32, Sign in 77 by 30, the sort dropdown 136 by 32, the cookie banner buttons 32 tall. Apple asks for 44, Google for 48. Nothing is unusable, but on a phone this is the difference between one tap and two.
- The CI gate only asserts one thing. It catches horizontal scroll and nothing else, which is exactly why the 768px headline collision has been sitting in an uploaded screenshot that nobody opened. Overlap, text spilling out of its container, and tap target size are all measurable in the same script for a few more lines.
- 1024px is never tested. layout-check.ts uses 390, 768 and 1440. The top bar switches from drawer to full nav at 1024 (`lg:hidden` on the menu button), so the width where the header layout actually changes is the one width not checked.
- The cookie banner eats 200px of an 844px phone screen, about 24% of the first view, and sits over the content until dismissed. On the home page that is the space where the first row of posters would be.

METHOD:
Rendered 10 routes at 4 widths plus 3 routes at 4 narrow phone widths in headless Chromium against a local dev server, measuring document overflow, tap target rectangles and headline text spill, and stepped the home page through 700 to 1280px to find the exact band where the hero breaks; also read LandingHero.tsx, TopBar.tsx, TopBarSearch.tsx, scripts/layout-check.ts and .github/workflows/layout.yml. Supabase is unreachable from this container, so title, person, collection and calendar pages rendered as error shells and their responsive behaviour is unverified.
