SCORE_AGREE: yes

SUMMARY:
The machinery is better than the paperwork. Analytics is genuinely gated: Google Analytics and PostHog are injected only after a visitor clicks "Accept all" (src/lib/analytics.ts:43 and src/lib/posthog.ts both return early unless getConsent() === "all"), the two banner buttons are visually identical so refusing costs the same as accepting (src/components/balasaur/CookieBanner.tsx:45-58), and signed-in users get real self-serve data export and account deletion, including the auth record (src/lib/account.functions.ts:55 calls supabaseAdmin.auth.admin.deleteUser). The published documents undo that. Both legal pages ship unfilled template placeholders to production: I rendered /privacy and /terms and counted three occurrences of [CONTACT EMAIL], two of [EFFECTIVE DATE], and one of [YOUR STATE/COUNTRY] in the served HTML. The contact address at src/routes/contact.tsx:6 is balasaur@ranklist.com, while the owner's domain is ranklist.io, so the only published way to reach a human is one letter off a domain someone else owns. Nowhere on the site is there a legal or personal name, a country of establishment beyond "operated from the United States", or a postal address, which is the identity disclosure GDPR Article 13 asks for and the reachable-owner check AdSense runs. Movie Night shipped 24 hours ago storing visitor-typed display names server-side (night_members.display_name, 14 rows in production); the privacy policy still says un-signed-in activity is "stored locally in your browser".

STRENGTHS:
- Consent is enforced, not decorative. Both trackers no-op until consent is "all", PostHog is loaded from CDN rather than bundled so a refusing visitor downloads nothing, and withdrawing consent calls stopPostHog() mid-session (src/components/balasaur/AnalyticsManager.tsx:29-40).
- The consent record stores choice, ISO timestamp and banner text version, expires after 365 days, and treats the old bare-string format as expired (src/lib/consent.ts:45-64). That is what a regulator actually asks for, and most solo sites store a single boolean.
- Subject rights are wired, not promised. /account offers a JSON export and a permanent delete that removes the Supabase auth user, so the "you can delete your account at any time" sentence in the policy is true.
- TMDB and OMDb attribution is present in the global footer on every page, plus JustWatch (src/components/balasaur/Footer.tsx). The required TMDB non-endorsement wording is verbatim.
- Movie Night was built privacy-aware: rooms carry noindex (src/routes/night.$code.tsx:51), expire 24 hours after creation, and a scheduled job purges them a day later (night-rooms-cleanup cron, movie_night_backbone.sql:551,566). Production shows 0 rooms past the purge window.

GAPS:
- The public contact email is on the wrong domain. src/routes/contact.tsx:6 publishes balasaur@ranklist.com; the owner has ranklist.io. Corrections, licensing, DMCA and takedown mail all go to a stranger or bounce. This is a one-character fix and it is the single highest-value item in this category.
- /privacy and /terms are live with template brackets in them. Three [CONTACT EMAIL], two [EFFECTIVE DATE], one [YOUR STATE/COUNTRY], verified in the rendered HTML. The rights section tells EU visitors to write to "[CONTACT EMAIL]". AdSense content review reads these two pages; unfilled placeholders read as an abandoned template.
- No controller identity anywhere. No name, no trading name, no country of establishment, no address. "Operated by Balasaur" is not a legal person. Article 13(1)(a) wants who, and the ad networks want to know a human is behind the domain.
- The privacy policy predates Movie Night and contradicts it. It says anonymous activity "may be stored locally in your browser"; night_members holds server-side display names typed by people who never made an account, alongside their genre picks. Nothing discloses the 24-hour retention that the code actually implements, which is a good story going untold.
- The privacy and terms pages are the only two pages with no route-level Footer of their own, so they inherit only the root footer, and the cookie banner does not render server-side. Nothing on /privacy links to the cookie control the policy tells the reader to use ("manage your choices through our cookie banner at any time", privacy.tsx:106). The footer's Cookie settings button is the only entry point.
- /about and /contact render the attribution footer twice (I counted two "provided by JustWatch" strings on /contact and three attribution blocks on /about) because those routes render <Footer /> on top of the one already in __root.tsx:206. Harmless legally, sloppy on the two pages a reviewer reads.
