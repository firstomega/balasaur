SCORE_AGREE: no, I would give 6.0
The read path is genuinely built to scale (every hot query is a primary-key lookup behind a 6-hour CDN cache), so what is broken here is cost and waste at zero traffic, not the ability to serve traffic when it arrives.

SUMMARY:
The database is 10,022 MB and 93% of it is storage for pages that do not exist. media_cache holds 1,010,038 rows of TMDB payloads at 6,858 MB, but only 72,554 of those ids match a title in the actual catalog, and nothing ever evicts the other 937,000: not one row in the table is older than 30 days, because the ingest loop keeps refreshing all of them. In the 11 days since the query counters were reset on 16 August, Postgres spent 30.1 hours executing queries while Search Console recorded zero clicks, and 19.5 of those hours (65% of all database time) went to a single function, related_titles, called 1,082,736 times at 64.8 ms each. That function is the "more like this" rail, and it was run roughly 97,000 times a day to build rails for detail pages nobody has ever loaded. The user-facing side is in much better shape: media_cache primary-key lookups ran 2.81 million times at 2.4 ms, person_cache 3.43 million times at 1.0 ms, and the buffer cache hit rate is 98.63%. At 1,000 daily visitors nothing breaks; at 50,000 the read path still holds, and the first things to give are the 60-connection ceiling shared with the ingest loop and the cold-cache long tail, where a title missing from media_cache costs two related_titles calls at about 220 ms each plus outbound TMDB and OMDb fetches.

STRENGTHS:
- The hot read path is a key-value lookup, not a query. Serving a title page reads media_cache by primary key: 2,810,881 index scans at 2.4 ms mean, and person_cache 5,207,333 scans at 1.0 ms. Combined with the 6-hour CDN cache on SSR pages, ordinary traffic barely touches Postgres.
- Expensive work is precomputed rather than done per request. person_index is a materialized view refreshed 9 times at 87 seconds each, rebuild_collections runs as a batch job at 23 seconds, and catalog_facets_cache is a single row. None of that is on a visitor's request.
- Filter columns that the catalog page actually uses are indexed and the indexes are being chosen: idx_media_popularity 269,070 scans, idx_media_rating_balasaur 108,663, idx_media_type 83,147, idx_media_genres 78,192, idx_media_themes 76,405. This is not a table full of unindexed WHERE clauses.
- Buffer cache hit rate is 98.63% across all tables despite a 10 GB database on a 256 MB shared_buffers instance, which means the hot working set is genuinely small and separable from the bulk.
- The Movie Night functions are cheap. night_state, which the room page polls, measured 1.0 ms mean and 12 ms worst case over 168 calls; night_create 9.5 ms.

GAPS:
- media_cache has no eviction and no relationship to what is published. 1,010,038 rows, 6,858 MB, of which 3,855 MB is the detail_payload JSON alone, and 937,484 rows correspond to no catalog title. Dropping rows with no matching media_id would take the database from 10,022 MB to roughly 3,500 MB. Supabase Pro includes 8 GB of disk and bills above it, and provisioned disk does not shrink on its own once grown.
- Two thirds of all database time is spent on pages that get no traffic. related_titles: 1,082,736 calls, 70,206 seconds total. Only 23 distinct URLs have ever surfaced in search. The rails are being generated at roughly 4,000 per hour, continuously, for a catalog that has 144 lifetime impressions.
- related_titles cannot be indexed out of its cost. It scans every row of media matching the target type, expands the people JSON array per candidate row, and sorts. Measured at 221 ms warm for Inception, touching 8,405 buffers. It is linear in catalog size, so the 220 ms becomes 2.2 seconds if media ever grows toward the 1 million rows media_cache implies is the ambition.
- media carries 27 indexes for 77,672 rows. 5 have never been scanned and cost 92 MB, but the disk is the small part: only 397 of 186,756 row updates were HOT (0.2%), so almost every nightly update rewrites 27 index entries. That is why a 77,672-row table occupies 2,320 MB and sits at 19.7% dead tuples with only 7 autovacuum passes in 11 days.
- Nothing throttles the ingest loop against the serving path. max_connections is 60, shared between PostgREST, the GitHub Actions enrichment loop, and the nightly jobs. Today the loop wins because there are no visitors; there is no priority mechanism for when that changes.
- The Movie Night room polls night_state every 12 seconds (src/routes/night.$code.tsx:136) and that request cannot be CDN-cached. Each call is 1 ms so Postgres is not the limit, but 500 simultaneous room members is 42 uncacheable requests per second against a 60-connection pool.
